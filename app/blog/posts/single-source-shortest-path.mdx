Imagine you are standing at **Connaught Place** in **Delhi**, and you open **Google Maps**. You want to find the 
shortest travel distance from your current location to different locations across **Delhi**.

<DelhiShortestPathVisualizer />

**Single Source Shortest Path (SSSP)** algorithms are the solution to this problem. They let you find the shortest 
distance from a given source vertex to every other vertex in a graph. 

Let's explore a few possible variations and understand how their approaches differ.

---

### Naive Approach: Bellmand-Ford

Consider the sub-graph between **Connaught Place** and **Lodhi Garden** containing `12` vertices and `15` edges.

<ShortestPathSubpathVisualizer />

1. The shortest path is composed of shortest paths to its intermediate vertices.

2. A shortest path never needs to repeat a vertex, provided there is no negative-weight cycle.
Therefore, it can contain at most `V-1` edges. Consequently, after `V-1` iterations, we are guaranteed 
to have discovered all shortest distances.
